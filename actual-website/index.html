Our Story

A Humble Beginning

Long before we transformed into a bustling modern community, the land where our barangay stands was a vast green space full of nature. Originally established as a small, quiet village reliant on agriculture and local trade, the community worked together closely, showing the true spirit of bayanihan by helping out its neighbors. This growth turned our quiet provincial village into a busy and lively neighborhood where more people moved and migrated to live and work.

Heritage

Growth, Resilience, and Progress

As years went by, our barangay faced many changes and challenges. We went through tough times, like strong typhoons and economic problems, but the people never gave up and always worked together to rebuild. Soon, the dirt roads were paved, schools were built, and small local businesses started opening up everywhere. This growth turned our quiet provincial village into a busy and lively neighborhood.

Today

Present Day and Moving Forward

Today, our barangay stands proudly as a dynamic and inclusive community that balances rapid modernization with a deep respect for its historical roots. Under a succession of dedicated local leaders, the barangay has continually upgraded its infrastructure, digital capabilities, and public services to meet the demands of the modern day. While the landscape has evolved from fields to thriving residential and commercial spaces, the core values of Compassion, Integrity, Nationalism, Commitment, and Order continue to guide the barangay toward a bright and sustainable future.

📋 Documents & Certificates

Visit the Barangay Hall during office hours (Mon–Fri, 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM) and bring the following:

  • One (1) valid government-issued ID with photo and address
  • Community Tax Certificate (Cedula) — current year
  • Duly accomplished request form (available at the hall)

Processing takes approximately 15–30 minutes. The fee is ₱50.00.

You can request the following documents at the Barangay Hall:

  • Barangay Clearance (₱50 · 15–30 mins)
  • Certificate of Residency (₱50 · 15–30 mins)
  • Barangay Indigency Certificate (Free · 20–30 mins)
  • Business Permit Clearance (₱200–₱500 · 30–60 mins)
  • Certificate of Good Moral Character (₱50 · 15–20 mins)
  • Barangay ID (₱100 · 3–5 working days)
  • Cedula / Community Tax Certificate (Minimum ₱5 · 10–20 mins)

To obtain a Certificate of Residency, bring:

  • Valid Government-Issued ID with your current Barangay CINCO address
  • Community Tax Certificate (Cedula) — current year
  • Proof of residency: utility bill, lease contract, or similar document

You must have resided in Barangay CINCO for at least 6 months. Fee: ₱50.00.

Present a valid ID and your previous year's Cedula at the Treasurer's window. The fee starts at ₱5.00 and may vary based on income. Processing takes 10–20 minutes. Must be renewed every year.

Community Safety

Disaster Preparedness

Know what to do before, during, and after a disaster. Be informed, be ready.

Before
  • Prepare a go-bag with essentials (food, water, medicines, documents)
  • Know your evacuation route and nearest evacuation center
  • Secure loose items outside your home
  • Stock up on at least 3 days of food and water
  • Charge all devices and power banks
  • Monitor PAGASA weather bulletins regularly
During
  • Stay indoors and away from windows
  • Do not go outside during the eye of the storm
  • Follow instructions from barangay officials
  • Evacuate immediately if ordered to do so
  • Never cross flooded roads or bridges
After
  • Check for injuries and provide first aid
  • Avoid damaged structures and downed power lines
  • Use safe drinking water only
  • Report damage to the barangay office
  • Help neighbors, especially seniors and PWDs
Before
  • Secure heavy furniture and appliances to walls
  • Know your home's safe spots (under sturdy tables)
  • Prepare emergency kit and keep it accessible
  • Practice earthquake drills with your household
  • Know how to shut off gas and electricity
During
  • DROP, COVER, and HOLD ON
  • Stay away from windows, shelves, and heavy objects
  • Do NOT run outside during shaking
  • If outdoors, move away from buildings and power lines
  • If driving, pull over away from structures
After
  • Expect aftershocks — stay alert
  • Check for gas leaks and structural damage
  • Evacuate if your building is unsafe
  • Use battery-powered radio for updates
  • Do not use elevators after an earthquake
Before
  • Know your flood risk zone and evacuation routes
  • Store important documents in waterproof containers
  • Prepare emergency supply kit
  • Keep drains and gutters clear
  • Monitor flood warnings from PAGASA
During
  • Move to higher ground immediately
  • Never walk or drive through floodwaters
  • Avoid contact with floodwater
  • Turn off electricity at the main breaker
  • Stay away from power lines and electrical wires
After
  • Return home only when authorities say it's safe
  • Clean and disinfect everything touched by floodwater
  • Throw away contaminated food
  • Watch for weakened structures and hazards
  • Report damage to barangay authorities
Prevention
  • Check electrical wiring regularly
  • Never leave cooking unattended
  • Keep flammable materials away from heat sources
  • Install smoke detectors and fire extinguishers
  • Create and practice a fire escape plan
During a Fire
  • Alert everyone and evacuate immediately
  • Crawl low under smoke
  • Feel doors before opening
  • Use stairs, never elevators
  • Call emergency hotline once safely outside
  • Never go back inside a burning building
If Caught in Fire
  • STOP, DROP, and ROLL
  • Cover nose and mouth with wet cloth
  • Stay close to the ground
  • Signal for help from a window if trapped
Before
  • Pack an emergency go-bag
  • Identify evacuation routes
  • Avoid building near steep slopes
  • Watch for warning signs like ground cracks
  • Prepare emergency supplies and documents
During
  • Evacuate immediately to higher ground
  • Avoid bridges and muddy roads
  • Seek shelter under sturdy furniture if trapped indoors
  • Protect head and neck if trapped outdoors
After
  • Do not return immediately
  • Watch for flooding and additional slides
  • Report damaged roads and utilities
💧 Stay hydrated before, during, and after heat wave
🕶️ Limit sun exposure during peak hours (10AM–4PM)
🏠 Prepare cooling measures at home
🌡️ Learn symptoms of heat exhaustion and heat stroke
🚿 Use cool showers and wet towels to reduce body temperature
🥤 Drink plenty of water — avoid caffeinated and alcoholic drinks
🏋️ Avoid heavy activities during the hottest hours of the day
🪟 Open windows only when outdoor temperature drops
👴 Check on elderly and sick people regularly

📖 How to Get Your Documents

Walk-in service at the Barangay Hall. Follow these four steps to get your requested document smoothly.

01

Prepare Requirements

Gather all required documents including a valid government-issued ID, current Cedula, and any supporting papers relevant to your request.

02

Visit the Barangay Hall

Head to the Barangay Hall during office hours (Mon–Fri, 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM). Proceed to the Records Window and fill out the request form.

03

Document Processing

Submit your requirements and official receipt at the Records Section. Processing typically takes 15–30 minutes during regular hours.

04

Release & Signing

Claim your signed document from the releasing window. Ensure all details are correct before leaving the premises.
